# Taste System — Hanna S. Dunham

## What Taste Means Here

Taste is the set of subjective decisions that separate a generic template from something that feels made. It is not a checklist. It is a coherent point of view applied consistently across every decision.

This document defines the taste for this project so that any future contribution — design, copy, animation, layout — can be evaluated against it.

---

## The Register

**Executive editorial.** The site exists at the intersection of two visual languages:

1. **Editorial** — the visual DNA of serious long-form media: *The Atlantic*, *Harvard Law Review*, architectural magazines. Characteristics: generous whitespace, serif display type, careful restraint, content-first layouts, no decoration for decoration's sake.

2. **Enterprise** — the visual DNA of serious software and financial firms: Palantir, Bloomberg Terminal UI, McKinsey decks. Characteristics: dark themes, high-information density where needed, systematic typography, muted accent colors.

The result is a site that feels like a premium product, not a personal brand.

---

## Reference Aesthetic

Sites and objects this project aspires to:

- Stripe's documentation dark mode — structured, trustworthy, excellent typography
- Vercel's marketing site — confident whitespace, sharp contrast hierarchy
- The Economist's digital design — editorial typography, authoritative tone
- Linear's marketing — motion restraint, depth without gimmick

Sites this project explicitly rejects:

- Generic Webflow resume templates (hero metrics, floating avatars, bright gradients)
- Startup founder pages (playful illustrations, waving emoji, casual type)
- Agency portfolio sites (overdone parallax, cursor effects, heavy JS)
- LinkedIn-style layouts (badge grids, progress bars, section emoji)

## The 12 Taste Rules

1. **One accent color, used sparingly.** Warm gold (#D4A843) appears on labels, dots, CTAs, and the credential suffix. It does not appear everywhere.

2. **Type does the work.** Hierarchy comes from size, weight, and spacing — not color blocks, borders, or backgrounds.

3. **White space is not wasted space.** Generous padding communicates confidence. Tight packing communicates anxiety.

4. **Dark means sophisticated, not edgy.** The dark background is zinc-950 (#09090B), not pure black. It has warmth.

5. **Animation is invisible when it's working.** If a visitor notices an animation, it is probably too much. The goal is a feeling of arrival, not a show.

6. **No decorative borders.** Side-stripe left borders on cards are a beginner pattern. Borders are for structure, not decoration.

7. **No gradient text.** Ever. It reads as template.

8. **Cards should not have cards inside them.** Avoid nested card patterns.

9. **Section labels carry the visual rhythm.** The small uppercase labels in accent color are the only repetitive decorative element. They anchor the page's vertical rhythm.

10. **Serif is reserved for personality moments.** Playfair Display appears on the name, section headings, and credential abbreviations (J.D., B.A.). Not on body copy, not on labels, not on tags.

11. **Motion serves content hierarchy.** The most important element on the hero (the name) should feel like the most important reveal. Stagger delays reinforce reading order.

12. **Mobile is not an afterthought.** The layout degrades gracefully — never just "squished desktop."

## What We Protect

Things that should never be changed without strong justification:

- The warm gold accent (the single accent rule)
- Generous section padding (the breathing room)
- Playfair Display for headings (the editorial register)
- The dark zinc-950 background (the dark-but-warm base)
- The quiet animation philosophy (no "wow, that's animated")
- The label pattern (small uppercase accent-colored labels above every section heading)

---

## What Can Evolve

- Individual section layouts (as long as the rhythm is preserved)
- The tagline (should be updated with Hanna's own words eventually)
- Number of competency categories
- Background gradient intensity (can be reduced if too much)
- The floating document frames (decorative — can be removed if distracting)
